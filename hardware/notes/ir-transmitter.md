# IR Transmitter

Four TSAL6200 IR LEDs (940nm) positioned at 0°, 45°, 90°, and 135° give
omnidirectional coverage for room-wide transmission — the spec requires
reliable communication across a 180° arc up to 10m, including via wall
reflections, rather than the precise aiming the breadboard prototype needed.

## LED drive

Each LED has its own current-limiting resistor from the 3.3V rail; all four
cathodes join at a single N-channel MOSFET drain so one GPIO switches the
whole array and all LEDs emit simultaneously.

- TSAL6200: Vf ≈ 1.5V at the 100mA target current.
- R = (3.3 − 1.5) / 0.1 = 18Ω per LED.
- Resistor dissipation: I²R = 0.1² × 18 = 0.18W → use 1/4W parts.
- Powered from 3.3V rather than 5V so less power is burned in the resistors.

## MOSFET driver

IRLML6344 logic-level N-FET sinks the combined 4 × 100mA = 400mA. Threshold
~1.0V, fully enhanced at 3.3V gate drive, Rds(on) ≈ 27mΩ at Vgs = 2.5V.

Gate network: 10kΩ series resistor (inrush/ESD) into the gate, 100kΩ
pull-down to ground so the array is guaranteed OFF while the GPIO is
high-impedance during boot/reset.

The IR carrier (38kHz) is generated by the ESP32 on GPIO18; the MOSFET just
switches it — there is no hardware modulator.

## Transmit indicator

A red LED with 470Ω series resistor (~3mA, deliberately dim) hangs directly
off the IR_TX signal in parallel with the gate drive, giving visible feedback
during transmission since 940nm is invisible to the eye.
